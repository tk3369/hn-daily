    Wax motor

    Wikipedia's wax motor page became a terminology fight: is a wax motor a motor (actuation by deliberate heating) or a thermostat (ambient expansion)? The page's photo of a thermostatic radiator valve is cited as a mislabel. Practical datapoints included: ~100 units in a hotel, one replaced every couple of years.

    • What it is
      • Paraffin-wax thermal actuator; reliable in damp environments
      • Photo allegedly shows a thermostatic valve, not a wax motor
    • HN take
      • Does 'motor' need a heater, or is ambient expansion enough?
      • Spatial vs temporal reading of 'thermal gradient'
    Source: en.wikipedia.org · Discuss: HN Discuss · 467 pts / 82 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe most HN comment possible: a Wikipedia correction thread arguing physics semantics. One camp: a wax motor must actuate — heat deliberately applied, cool to retract — while thermostatic valves are ambient at both ends, so the radiator-valve photo is a mislabel. The other: expansion happens from absolute temperature alone, no gradient needed, so 'motor' just means 'creates movement.' Meta-layer: the two sides may mean different things by 'thermal gradient' (spatial vs temporal). Empirical interlude: one commenter services ~100 in a hotel (replaces one every couple years), another replaced half their floor-heating units in a decade — so 'rarely fails' gets disputed.
