    Claude finds a CRISPR-like enzyme system

    Anthropic says Claude discovered a novel enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats — a genuine biology finding produced by an AI agent, not a benchmark. If the underlying work holds up in peer review, it's a concrete data point for LLM-driven science. That's a big if, and the paper is the proof.

    • What happened
      • Anthropic announcement: novel enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats, attributed to Claude
      • No published paper yet — that's the missing piece
    • HN take
      • Half excited, half skeptical: 'in a year this is either peak hype or the start of the singularity'
      • Top fear: another lab is about to publish the same finding and Claude proofread their paper
      • Biology is harder for LLMs than math — Anthropic scoped the problem down a lot; publish or it didn't happen
    Source: anthropic.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 706 pts / 721 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingCommenters split between real excitement and genuine skepticism. The missing paper is the main complaint: one wag predicted a lab will complain next week that Claude proofread their manuscript on the same finding and 'whoops, how'd that get into Anthropic's training data.' Others note biology is a harder problem for LLMs than math — Anthropic had to scope it down from something ambitious — and that finding patterns in huge datasets is exactly what LLMs do well. Consensus: cool, now publish the results.

    VSCode's SSH agent is bananas

    Fly.io's breakdown of VSCode's remote SSH agent: a WebSocket tunnel back to your local machine that can roam the filesystem, edit files, and spawn shells. Remote-dev fans call it a godsend; the Emacs/Tramp crowd notes the scary direction is a compromised remote owning your local box.

    • The mechanism
      • WebSocket back to your running VSCode front-end; can wander the filesystem, edit files, launch PTY shells
      • Security-world name for tools that work this way went politely unsaid
    • HN take
      • Fans: restrict SSH access arbitrarily; never been an issue in practice
      • Skeptics: remote-typing is fine, the reverse direction — compromised remote → local machine — is not acceptable
      • Tramp did the same thing first, and the spiritual forebearer gets its due
    Source: fly.io · Discuss: HN Discuss · 266 pts / 163 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread is a proxy war between remote-dev pragmatists and security minimalists. One side: VSCode SSH is a godsend for teams, guardrails exist, use them. The other: the agent-only-types-what-you-could-type model is acceptable, but the reverse tunnel means a compromised remote can do anything to your local machine — and that direction is not acceptable. The Emacs faction mostly enjoyed watching Tramp get its flowers as the spiritual forebearer.

    • HN take

      • 'If I see a single author post-2023, I assume it's junk'

    Source: blog.arxiv.org · Discuss: HN Discuss · 231 pts / 30 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingRelief about the funding, fury about the spam. Commenters agreed the money is sorely needed — 'how is science to evolve if good research requires $49 a pop?' — but the dominant sub-thread was arXiv being polluted by AI-generated papers: editors can't keep up, post-2023 solo authors get assumed junk, and one reader reported reviewers themselves using LLMs. Others worried about opinion pieces laundered as research through arXiv's credibility. No magic bullets offered.
