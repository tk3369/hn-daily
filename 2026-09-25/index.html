    Why is the liver so weirdly regenerative?

    A dynomight essay on the liver's strange superpower: it regrows to the right mass, not the original shape. The comment section delivered the goods — a working pathologist showed up, recommended Weinberg's The Biology of Cancer, and the thread dove into vitamin A toxicity science.

    • The essay
      • Walks through how liver regeneration actually works and why it's odd among organs
    • HN take
      • A pathologist gave a reading list and bought-in credibility; commenters are ordering the book
      • Vitamin A toxicity tangent: provitamin carotenes from food can't cause hypervitaminosis A — the conversion is tightly regulated
      • Serum retinol does not correlate with liver stores at all — a useful, non-obvious fact
    Source: dynomight.substack.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 466 pts / 238 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingOne of HN's best science threads. The pathologist's arrival turned it into a mini-lecture with book recommendations, and the vitamin A subthread was unusually well-cited: carotenes from vegetables and fruit are self-limiting by enzymatic regulation, while preformed retinol (dairy, meat fats, supplements, cod liver oil) is the real toxicity risk. Disagreement stayed factual — people asked for the actual papers and got them.

The thread's consensus lands where most good content strategy does: publish both forms.

    Fearless SIMD v1.0

    Linebender's portable-SIMD crate hit 1.0 — and it's what finally let the PhastFT FFT crate run on stable Rust, no nightly std::simd required. The thread is a SIMD-wonk rabbit hole: NaN-aware min/max codegen, pattern types, and a Dart compiler dev asking about interprocedural float analyses.

    • Why it matters
      • Stable Rust SIMD without nightly — a real blocker for production crates is gone
      • PhastFT's migration story is the proof: they needed features, the maintainer built them
    • HN take
      • Deep technical digression: x86 min vs minimumnum, LLVM nnan flags, range analysis on floats
      • Realistic pushback: knowing a float is never NaN ahead of time is nearly impossible outside trivial cases
      • Julia folks: possible there without patching the compiler, via compiler-extension packages
    Source: linebender.org · Discuss: HN Discuss · 277 pts / 43 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingA small, dense thread of people who actually write SIMD code. The PhastFT story is the heart of it: the crate's author evaluated portable SIMD options, picked fearless_simd, hit missing functionality, and one of the maintainers ended up contributing significantly to the 1.0 — open-source dogfooding at its best. The rest is pure compiler talk: whether interprocedural analysis can ever track custom float subsets, and the honest answer that NaN-tracking is almost always impossible past initialization.
