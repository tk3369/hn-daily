    Utah's VPN age-verification law blocked: court agrees with EFF

    A federal judge preliminarily enjoined Utah SB 73's VPN provisions, the first state law targeting VPN use to evade age-verification gates. EFF's argument, adopted by the court: the law demands perfect geolocation that does not exist, forcing sites to either block all VPN traffic nationwide or verify every visitor worldwide. Aylo (Pornhub parent) sued; enforcement was set for May 2026, proposed rules due Oct 8.

    • The law
      • SB 73: block VPN users or identify their physical location; ban VPN-bypass instructions
      • First state to target VPN evasion of age-verification gates
      • Preliminary injunction: likely violates dormant Commerce Clause
    • The technical problem
      • VPNs expose only the exit server IP — Salt Lake City, Seattle, or Shanghai looks identical
      • State rules demanded 'commercially reasonable' obfuscation detection
      • Latency/time-zone heuristics: unreliable, easily skewed by normal networks
    • HN take
      • 'Lawfare against porn' vs 'lawfare against protected speech' — Miller obscenity doctrine argued both ways
      • Top technical thread: self-hosted proxies make VPN detection pointless
      • 'For the children' framing vs trafficking/religious objections, largely pro-privacy room
    Source: eff.org · Discuss: HN Discuss · 691 pts / 326 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe room is broadly pro-privacy but argumentative about doctrine. The leading technical point is that reliable VPN detection is a losing game: anyone can proxy through a random hosting provider or self-host, so the law selects for the least detectable users while burdening everyone else. The legal subthread splits over whether pornography is protected speech — one side cites Miller v. California for the obscenity exception, the other replies that porn is rarely obscene under that very test. A darker reading recurs: the impossibility is the point, lawfare designed to make compliance untenable and shut operators down. The sincerity of the child-protection rationale is contested from both directions, including the claim that the real targets are women performers put out of work by prohibition. The EFF framing — perfection demanded, perfection unavailable — is the one nearly everyone accepts as factually correct.

    FLUX 3 Image: draw boxes, the model fills them

    Black Forest Labs' FLUX 3 Image generates and edits images from explicit spatial layouts: you place bounding boxes on a 0–1000 grid, describe each element, and the model renders inside your boxes — or an LLM plans the layout from one line. Also: multi-reference composition (up to 10 refs), native 2K/4K output, pixel-perfect multi-box edits that leave untouched regions alone, and commercial weights for scale deployers.

    • The interface
      • Bounding-box layout: [y_min,x_min,y_max,x_max] per element, ids cited in the caption
      • Prompt upsampler writes the dense caption around your boxes verbatim
      • Or skip boxes: plain text-to-image with strong prompt following
    • Editing and output
      • Multiple targeted edits at once; untouched areas preserved exactly
      • Up to 10 reference images composed into one scene
      • Native 2K and 4K rendering; commercial weights license available
    • HN take
      • InvokeAI/Ideogram déjà vu: Ideogram did boxes in June, via cumbersome JSON
      • ComfyUI wars: agents now wire node graphs so humans don't have to
      • Sprite-sequence question: frame-coherent generation still unsolved
    Source: bfl.ai · Discuss: HN Discuss · 374 pts / 82 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe feature is recognized immediately as a direction several labs are converging on — Ideogram shipped box-based layout in June with a JSON interface HN found cumbersome, and Adobe's Generative Fill gets cited as the inpainting cousin. The practical HN workflow has already moved past hand-drawing boxes: commenters have LLMs emit the layout JSON, agents drive ComfyUI's API directly (to general agreement that Comfy's node UI is uniquely hostile), and one poster had an LLM rebuild their whole Qwen pipeline outside ComfyUI for a measured 20% speedup by caching text embeddings and fusing projections. The unanswered need is temporal: coherent frame-by-frame sprite sequences defeat every current image model, and the workarounds — reference frame, short video, extract frames — top out around indie fidelity. FLUX 3's boxes control space, not time; the thread noticed.

    Four exoplanets, 12 years, one movie

    A Bluesky post shares a 12-year sequence of Keck Telescope images of HR 8799, a star 133 light-years away: four planets, each more massive than Jupiter, directly imaged crawling along their orbits. The movie composites 10 frames taken 2010–2021 (Jason Wang / William Thompson / Christian Marois). Direct imaging — photographing the planet, not inferring it — remains the hardest trick in exoplanet science.

    • The system
      • HR 8799: 133 light-years, four directly imaged super-Jupiters
      • 10 Keck images, 2010–2021, composited into an orbital movie
      • Planets crawl: outer orbits take decades; 'those far out orbits sure take a long time'
    • What you are seeing
      • Not raw video: ~10 stills plus Kepler-law motion interpolation
      • Flickering red glow near the star: diffraction artifacts pulled by interpolation
      • Drake-equation footnote: fraction of stars with planets now near 100%
    • HN take
      • Interpolation caveat stated plainly and appreciated, not buried
      • Next hope: Roman Coronagraph, 100–1,000× better contrast than current space scopes
      • Starship payload debate: Pez dispenser vs swarm mirrors vs SLS
    Source: bsky.app · Discuss: HN Discuss · 325 pts / 61 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingWonder, then immediate and welcome pedantry. The clarifications come from the makers themselves: this is 10 still images, one every year or so, with a few hundred interpolated frames generated from Kepler's laws — the smooth motion is computed, not photographed, and the red smoke flickering near the star is diffraction artifact dragged around by that interpolation. HN's verdict is that the caveat makes it better, not worse. The forward-looking thread is instrumental: the Nancy Grace Roman Coronagraph should image reflected light from Jupiter-analogs 100 million times fainter than their stars, and the launch-vehicle argument follows — Starship's Starlink-sized dispenser slot, hypothetical swarm mirrors latching together in orbit, shuttle-style bay doors on later variants, or simply SLS with an 8-meter ATLAST-class mirror. Nobody disputes the destination; the argument is purely about the truck.

    Aging is cells forgetting what they are: two papers, one model

    Eric Topol surveys two new papers — Gladyshev (Harvard) in Nature and Izpisua Belmonte (Altos Labs) in Cell — proposing that aging is driven substantially by loss of cell identity, not just accumulated damage. Cells sit in Waddington valleys held by a 3-tier epigenetic grammar; the slow layer (PRC2 chromatin architecture) erodes under chronic stress, cells drift mesenchymal (scar, inflammation), and epigenetic clocks turn out to measure that erosion.

    • The model
      • 3-tier grammar: fast stress response (minutes), state transitions (days), identity lock (PRC2)
      • With age the valleys shallow — decanalization — and cells drift mesenchymal
      • Mesenchymal drift verified across 46 tissue types; linked to atherosclerosis to Alzheimer's
    • Interventions
      • Caloric restriction: conceptually freezes drift; primate data inconsistent, impractical at scale
      • Partial reprogramming (OSKM/OSK short bursts): restores identity, cancer is the risk
      • Lithium via GSK3β: candidate for preserving neuron identity
    • HN take
      • Embryos as proof of reversibility — but fail-fast selection does the quality control
      • Adult therapy must rejuvenate in place, no discarding bad cells wholesale
      • 'Knees hurt because they forgot they were knees' — 291 points says it resonates
    Source: erictopol.substack.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 291 pts / 125 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread's center of gravity is the embryo argument: fertilization strips age-linked methylation, rebuilds telomeres, and clears damaged proteins — the strongest existence proof that epigenetic aging is reversible. The cold water follows immediately: embryogenesis is a ruthless fail-fast filter that discards what doesn't work, and an adult therapy enjoys no such luxury — it must rejuvenate fixed cells in place, without breaking tissue function or tipping cells into pluripotency and cancer, with 'much less room for error.' A metabolic middle path gets floated (mild stress and caloric restriction as cellular quality control, letting replacement do the work), and the mitochondria footnote is properly nerdy: the reset mostly swaps in the mother's dormant mitochondrial population rather than rejuvenating the father's. The joke that outperforms the science — knees hurting because they forgot they were knees and decided they were sentient cheese — is, structurally, exactly what the papers claim.
