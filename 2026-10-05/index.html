    Strata claims a 125B Qwen on one RTX 4090 at 100 tokens/s

The trick is aggressive sub-4-bit quantization; whether the quality holds is the whole argument.

    • The method

    • HN take

    Source: github.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 849 pts / 383 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread is a benchmark fight with unusually good data. A skeptic opens with the standard objection — below 4-bit you pay in quality — and cites a rented RTX Pro 6000 doing 1.2M output tokens/hour as the sane baseline. Then a commenter posts a head-to-head: Strata's IQ3 build beats a Q4/Q5 ninfer build 114/128 to 92/128 on coding tasks, while running slower wall-clock (142 vs 122 min) and with fewer API failures. The most HN moment is diagnostic: a user who let their local model debug why their PCIe link was stuck at 4x and talked it up to 16x. Belief is conditional, and the condition is reproduction.

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread is two kinds of exhaustion arguing with each other.     F1 cars died on the formation lap. The fix was a software patch.

    At the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia (Sepang), a software bug in the FIA-supplied energy management left multiple cars powerless on the wet formation lap — Verstappen first, Hamilton stopped and restarted on the pit straight, eight cars crawling. The FIA blamed low engine speed, low grip, wet-weather settings, and the circuit's sector layout draining batteries with turbos unspooled; a patched energy limit, uploaded in the pits, delayed the start over 90 minutes. Drivers called it 'horrible,' 'sad,' 'totally unacceptable.'

    • The failure
      • FIA software bug; multiple cars lost power on formation lap
      • Cause: revs too low behind safety car, battery drained, turbo unspooled
      • Hamilton's career-first: stopped on track, restarted the power unit
    • The fix
      • All cars back to pits; FIA patch removed the sector energy limit
      • Start delayed >90 minutes (past the rain delay itself)
      • FIA apology and a promised full review with teams and PU makers
    • HN take
      • Field-patching a critical controller 'seems wild' vs F1's perpetual QA
      • Budget-cap rebuttal: small teams update continuously too
      • 'Probably just settings, rather than an actual code update'
    Source: motorsport.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 252 pts / 216 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingEngineers read this as a process story. The first reaction is disbelief that a critical controller gets coded and deployed in the field without lab QA and root-cause — and the rebuttal is that F1 cars live in perpetual QA, modified between every race, which is precisely how a wet-weather edge case (slow laps, low revs, battery drain) ships undetected. The budget-cap correction matters: smaller teams aren't just 'happy to be there' anymore; everyone brings upgrades all year, with extra testing time deliberately granted to the back of the grid. The deflationary take may be the accurate one: the 'software update' was likely a settings change — an energy-limit flag unclipped — which makes the 90-minute delay less a deployment story than a testing one. Nobody tested the rain mode.

    The Tao of Backup, reread in the age of NixOS and snapshots

    The Tao of Backup — the koan-style backup parable ('the master made no reply') — is back on the front page, and in 2026 it reads as a requirements doc: what do you back up, where does the only uncorrupted copy live, and what did you forget (your age keys) that makes the restore worthless? The text is old; the failure modes are current.

    • The parable
      • Koans: the novice who'd reinstall from distribution disks (3 days later...)
      • The master who mailed a backup tape to his brother in China
      • 'Now you are truly enlightened' — yes, it's also a Veracity ad
    • 2026 answers
      • NixOS: back up one config file, recreate the system in minutes
      • Snapshots over backups; VMs with a clean host
      • The gotcha: sops/age keys outside the repo, forgotten, unrestorable
    • HN take
      • Bit-for-bit claims nitpicked: 'identical enough for all practical purposes'
      • Restores as cleanup opportunities vs bare-metal recovery discipline
      • 'You only learn after a catastrophic loss' — the skin-burned koan
    Source: taobackup.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 193 pts / 71 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread treats the parable as a design review. The NixOS camp answers the oldest koan directly: don't back up applications at all — back up the single declarative config and rebuild bit-for-bit (well, 'identical enough'; the nitpick is immediate and correct). Then the same camp indicts itself: the age keys your secrets live under sit outside the git repo, and forgetting them is the modern version of overwriting the only good tape. The traditionalists hold their ground — bare-metal recovery beats reinstalling, snapshot the hypervisor too — and the koan-keepers quote the master back at everyone: you only learn to back up after the catastrophic loss, and protecting the novice from it deprives them of the knowledge burned into the skin.
