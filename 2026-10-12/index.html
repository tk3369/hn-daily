    Passkeys are a trap: attestation lets sites lock you to approved vendors, and banks are first in line

    The argument: passkeys fix phishing, but WebAuthn attestation lets a site demand proof your credential came from an approved authenticator — and reject it if it did not. Attestation is requested at registration, and once sites require it, self-managed or open-source authenticators can be shut out. Japan is the worked example in the thread: the FSA required brokerages to implement passkeys, and users report hardware FIDO2 keys refused, with only Windows Hello or Apple's Passwords app accepted.

    • The mechanism
      • Attestation is obtained at credential registration; a site can later reject non-attested passkeys and force re-registration
      • Browsers show a permission prompt when a site requests attestation — the gate exists, sites choose whether to use it
    • Why it matters
      • The risk is not passkeys themselves but vendor lock-in by policy: who decides which authenticator you may use
      • Finance moves first — regulated, fraud-averse, and able to mandate the authenticator along with the login
    Source: nonfunctional.substack.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 789 pts / 428 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread tests the claim rather than accepting it. The top comments correct the mechanics — attestation is a registration-time decision, though a site can reject a passkey later and demand an attested one — and split on likelihood: one camp notes US banks barely manage SMS 2FA and ignore 3DS, so mandated attestation looks unlikely; the other answers that the same banks already demand mobile attestation for payments. The Japan brokerage reports are the concrete evidence: passkeys required, personal hardware keys refused.

    Total Annihilation, rebuilt for modern computers: Nanolathe is an open-source engine with a playable browser demo

    Nanolathe is an open-source, MIT-licensed 2.5D RTS engine that rebuilds Total Annihilation for modern hardware: a modern GPU renderer with light and glow (Classic look switchable), native builds for Windows, Mac and Linux using the game files you already own, and a browser demo with three original missions — no game folder or installation needed, desktop Chrome, Edge or Safari with keyboard and mouse. Single-player is ready; online play uses your own files.

    • What ships
      • Browser demo: three original demo missions, no installation; native beta for the full game
      • Modern GPU rendering standard, Classic rendering optional; code, documented formats and clean-room research open to study and extend
    • Why it matters
      • Preservation by reimplementation: the classic stays playable and hackable instead of rotting on abandonware
      • An engine, not just a port — others can build on the formats and research
    Source: nanolathe.gg · Discuss: HN Discuss · 581 pts / 221 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingNostalgia, immediately redirected: the top comments champion Beyond All Reason, the open-source spiritual successor, as the best modern way to play this lineage — polished, huge battles, weekly bot-bashes against the AI, and a community that will not flame a new player for holding the backline and doing economy. The Nanolathe thread and the BAR thread agree on the diagnosis: TA's exponential economy and fragile, game-driving buildings still make a distinctive RTS, and it has never really been replaced.

    Karpathy: the gap in shared understanding of LLM capability is widening — insiders and the public are diverging

    Andrej Karpathy's post, shared via X, argues the shared understanding of what LLMs can actually do is breaking down: people working with frontier models daily see capability the general public — and much of the professions — has not registered, while others over-trust the same systems. The HN discussion runs with the consequences: office workers who tried a chatbot once, a year ago, still believe their simple data-entry-adjacent jobs cannot be automated, while clients increasingly value being told they are right over being right.

    • The claim
      • Capability perception now varies more by exposure than by the models themselves; daily users and one-time triallers inhabit different realities
      • The linked post is an X post by Karpathy; discussion centres on professional work, not benchmarks
    • Why it matters
      • Mispricing cuts both ways: jobs automated later than expected, and judgement work quietly devalued now
      • The scarce skill shifts from doing the technical work to interpreting needs and checking outputs
    Source: twitter.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 543 pts / 785 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingAt 785 comments this is the day's biggest thread, and it argues about the premise: one camp doubts any internal class of users is far ahead of top public users — internal models are more eager and run at larger scale, but value still requires skill. The lived-experience camp reports the opposite from non-tech offices, where staff tried ChatGPT once and concluded their jobs are safe. The sharpest worry is not automation itself but incentives: decision-makers preferring an AI that agrees with them to a person who is right.

    NYC's Click to Cancel rule is now in effect: cancelling a subscription must be as easy as signing up

    New York City's Click to Cancel rule is now in effect, requiring that cancelling a subscription be as straightforward as starting one — the local version of the principle the federal rule pursued. The HN reality check lands immediately: cancellation flows can comply on the surface while the contract still bites, as with Adobe, where cancelling is clickable but an early-termination charge equal to the remaining annual term applies, on the stated logic that the annual discount was consideration for the commitment.

    • The rule
      • In effect in NYC: cancellation as easy as enrolment, aimed at dark-pattern retention flows
      • It governs the cancellation process; contract terms like annual-commitment fees are a separate fight
    • Why it matters
      • Process rules change the default: retention by friction stops being free
      • Expect the gap between easy-to-cancel and cheap-to-cancel to be where companies defend revenue
    Source: nyc.gov · Discuss: HN Discuss · 527 pts / 261 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingAdobe dominates, as expected: commenters recount discovering an annual commitment only at cancellation, with the fee framed as repayment of the discount. The thread then wanders productively into alternatives — Affinity (now Canva-owned, folded into one app, free as an account-and-upsell vehicle, in one account) and a Rust-built, offline, open-source Creative Suite alternative in progress, which splits readers between enthusiasm and vibe-coded scepticism. The consensus on the rule itself: necessary, and not sufficient.

    Fingerprints are the new cookies: tracking moved to signals you cannot clear, and consent law already covers it

    Browser fingerprinting — canvas, fonts, hardware and behaviour signals combined into an identifier — does the tracking job cookies did, without anything stored on your machine to delete. The post's legal point, sharpened in the thread: consent law was never about cookies specifically. EU consent is required for tracking and profiling however it is done; the banner is just the mechanism sites chose, and session cookies needed for authentication never required consent in the first place.

    • The shift
      • No stored token: identification is computed from device and browser signals, so clearing cookies does nothing
      • Harder to detect and block than cookies; blocking the signals can itself become a signal
    • The law, per the thread
      • Consent attaches to tracking/profiling, not to the cookie technology; Global Privacy Control is an honoured opt-out signal under CCPA and some state laws
      • Header-based preferences (DNT, then GPC) historically failed because opt-outs at scale are what the industry designs around
    • Why it matters
      • Privacy controls built around stored state miss computed identity entirely
      • The fight moves to browser defaults and signals, not banner text
    Source: royfish.dev · Discuss: HN Discuss · 508 pts / 250 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread corrects the popular history first: banners were never legally about cookies as such — tracking is the regulated act, and a site could defer consent to a natural moment like account creation instead of a hostile first-visit banner. From there it is mechanism design: DNT died because universal opt-out was foreseeable, GPC survives as a legal opt-out in some jurisdictions but no browser surfaces it prominently, and one campaign simply lobbies representatives to ban the banners outright. Nobody defends fingerprinting; they argue about which lever has ever worked.

    Terence Tao, Math 2.0: what happens to proof, understanding and trust when AI does the mathematics [pdf]

    Terence Tao's Caltech slides, 'Math 2.0', survey mathematics absorbing AI: machine-assisted proof, AI-generated arguments nobody fully understands, and the epistemic question underneath — if an AI-produced drug or proof passes every test, what exactly is the understanding we are missing, and does it matter? The drug-discovery slide draws the day's fire: practitioners say real drug mechanisms are already woefully incomplete, and a therapy that passed phase 3 trials would be prescribed regardless of explanatory depth.

    • The material
      • Slide deck (PDF) from teorth.github.io, Tao's own site: Math 2.0, Caltech 2026
      • Central tension: verification by test versus understanding by explanation, in maths and beyond
    • Why it matters
      • Mathematics is the test case because it has the strongest verification culture — if trust shifts there, it shifts everywhere
      • The failure mode Tao pictures is process-gaming: an AI exploiting weaknesses in the trial or review process itself
    Source: teorth.github.io · Discuss: HN Discuss · 442 pts / 451 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingDomain experts push back on the example, not the question: drug-discovery commenters say causal mechanisms for many effective drugs are already short of any first-principles explanation, and a stage-3-passing AI drug would face no hesitation. The defence of Tao's point is narrower and stronger — he pictures the AI exploiting the trial process itself, even hacking the regulator, not merely lacking an explanation. A third strand is unimpressed by both: humans gaming the letter of processes is the oldest behaviour in the thread.

    • Why it matters

Personal anecdotes of 'too old, no records' answers to legitimate account queries ground the abstraction.

    QGIS bans AI-assisted contributions: the bottleneck is no longer writing code, it is knowing what the code does

    QGIS, the open-source GIS project, moves to ban AI-assisted contributions (QEP proposal, GitHub). The maintainer logic the thread converges on: reviewing a large PR from a stranger was already the scarce resource, and AI multiplies submission volume while weakening the one signal review relied on — that the author understood their own code. The top comment puts it as an alignment problem on the human side: the bottleneck is no longer writing code but staying aligned with it, knowing what it does and whether it does what you meant.

    • The proposal
      • QGIS Enhancement Proposal on GitHub (QGIS-Enhancement-Proposals #387) to ban AI-assisted contributions
      • Framed as attention rationing: maintainers cannot verify what submitters themselves may not understand
    • Why it matters
      • A major project choosing reviewability over throughput is a measurable policy signal, not a vibe
      • The same argument is coming to every project whose maintainers review strangers' code for free
    Source: github.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 217 pts / 194 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingSympathy, mostly, with the diagnosis generalised: several developers say their own job has already become building the harness that builds the product, and that knowing what your software does was always thinner than admitted — legacy systems nobody fully understands predate LLMs by decades. The disagreement is over remedy and trajectory: is a ban proportionate rationing or a losing stance, and will deep codebase knowledge carry any monetary or reputational reward outside a niche? One commenter, mid-way through a Claude-written app, hopes to be wrong about the answer.

    Will Linux phones become a viable alternative to the Android/iOS duopoly? The thread's answer starts in China

    An October 5 essay asks whether Linux phones can break the Android/iOS duopoly. The thread's first move is to reframe the count: HarmonyOS already runs on a claimed 100 million devices in China with native versions of everyday apps, making the market arguably three-way — except HarmonyOS 5+ is not Linux at all, having replaced the earlier AOSP-based, multi-kernel design with Huawei's own microkernel. It exists because US sanctions forced a new stack into existence in a large, protected home market.

    • The state of play
      • HarmonyOS: large device base in China, native app ecosystem, iOS-comparable smoothness per a half-year user — and a bespoke non-Linux kernel
      • Linux phones proper remain the niche the essay describes; no comparable app-ecosystem breakthrough is reported
    • Why it matters
      • The one demonstrated route past the duopoly was state pressure plus a huge home market, not openness
      • A third app ecosystem, even a closed one, trains developers to build beyond two platforms — the spillover Linux phones actually need
    Source: amosbbatto.wordpress.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 214 pts / 236 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingTaxonomy first: commenters correct the premise that HarmonyOS counts as Linux, tracing versions 1–4.3 (AOSP-integrated) to the current microkernel system with its own languages, ArkTS and Cangjie. Then the verdict splits on values: proof that a new stack can succeed and weaken the duopoly, versus 'even less open than Android — not an improvement'. Nobody in the thread reports a Linux phone as their daily driver breakthrough; the ecosystem problem — apps, notifications, polish — is treated as the whole game.
