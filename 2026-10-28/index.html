    Ghostty 1.4: first non-Kitty terminal with a 100% complete Kitty graphics implementation, animation included

    Ghostty 1.4.0, released October 27, 2026, is seven months of work from 172 contributors across 2,889 commits. Headline items: large performance and memory improvements, a complete Kitty graphics implementation including animation and relative placements — the first non-Kitty terminal to do so, after a full conformance pass against the spec and Kitty itself — SSH integration improvements, and macOS 27 support. The notes also carry a stack of security fixes for hostile terminal output: paste protection that missed carriage returns, Kitty graphics commands that could crash the terminal or touch files outside allowed temp directories, and tmux control-mode crashes that needed no tmux installed.

    • What shipped
      • Kitty graphics: frame transmission, composition, playback control and relative placements; animations pause while the terminal is not visible
      • Security: fixes for paste-protection bypass via carriage returns, malicious graphics/tmux-control output, and macOS cmd-click on crafted OSC 8 links
    • Why it matters
      • Terminals render untrusted output all day — several of these bugs triggered on display alone, no click needed
      • A complete second implementation of the Kitty graphics protocol makes it a real standard instead of one program’s feature
    Source: ghostty.org · Discuss: HN Discuss · 1148 pts / 366 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingLoyalty and defection reports alternate. Long-time users call it snappy and settled; one says most of their work has shifted into Emacs via ghostel, built on libghostty, whose progress a maintainer stops by to highlight. The sceptical line is portability: WezTerm users stay for Windows support and one shared config across machines, and a Konsole user asks what any of the new terminals offers over it, missing only iTerm2-style tmux -CC integration. Two smaller threads: a user who left over strange SSH behaviour hopes this release fixes it and lets them ditch iTerm2, and another hopes no LLM features get added — “it’s honestly perfect as it is, just nerd performance tweaks.” A question to Mitchell Hashimoto about the promised move away from GitHub sits in the captured comments without an answer.

    Genie 4: DeepMind’s world model generates native audio and dialogue, with Gemini steering the simulation

    Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 on October 27: a real-time interactive world model that is natively multimodal — music, ambient sound and interactive dialogue generated together with the world. Gemini Omni anchors appearance and behaviour, and Gemini 3.8 Flash sits in the loop as a guiding agent, watching frames, breaking goals into steps and checking each step landed; a robotics experiment pairs it with Gemini 4 Argon to steer an Apptronik Apollo humanoid through a game of checkers inside the simulation. Rollout is a research preview for trusted testers, then Google AI Ultra subscribers via Project Genie and early-access Gemini API / AI Studio partners. DeepMind states the limits itself: object permanence and visual detail still drift over longer sessions and after many prompt edits.

    • The claims

      • All generated video and audio carries SynthID watermarking; testers so far span film pre-visualization, photojournalism and game prototyping
    • Why it matters
      • The pitch is training environments: infinitely variable, safe simulations for robots and agents before real-world trials
      • Stated limitations are the honest part — coherence holds over short sessions, not yet over long ones

    Python’s experimental JIT is on its way out: PEP 836 withdrawn after the steering council asked for guarantees

    LWN reports (October 26) that CPython’s experimental just-in-time compiler, merged by Brandt Bucher in early 2024 for the 3.13 release under the informational PEP 744, is to be removed. In June the steering council paused JIT work on the main branch until a standards-track PEP addressed maintenance, compatibility, clear success metrics and a timeline. The maintainers wrote that PEP — 836, “JIT Go Brrr” — and, after months of discussion, have now withdrawn it. The council’s stated worry was interference: getting the JIT to play well with instrumentation, debugging and profiling, alongside external JITs such as CinderX and JIT-adjacent tools like Numba and PyTorch’s compile. Subscriber-link story; the headline facts are in LWN’s free summary.

    • The sequence
      • 2024: JIT lands experimentally for 3.13 without a standards-track PEP; PEP 744 documents it after the fact
      • June 2026: council pauses the work pending a real PEP; October 2026: PEP 836 withdrawn, development effectively ends
    • Why it matters
      • Process outcome, not just a performance one: a major feature merged first and justified later did not survive being asked for guarantees
      • The performance problem does not go away — the answers remain the specialist tools: PyPy, Numba, Cython, C extensions
    Source: lwn.net · Discuss: HN Discuss · 545 pts / 297 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingBittersweet is the dominant register. Several commenters are tired of rewriting Python hot paths in Cython or Rust and had pinned hopes on the Faster CPython era; one notes part of the JIT’s value was offsetting free-threading’s performance penalty, and asks whether free-threading is next in doubt. The defence is equally concrete: the PEP process and its bureaucracy are what keep Python stable, the JIT’s measured gains were always modest, and working alternatives already exist. The sharpest objection quotes the discussion thread back: the PEP was expected to make the JIT team responsible for solving every downstream tool’s compatibility — “absurdly impossible, to the point of willfully killing the project.” Verdict in-thread: a reasonable outcome, reached expensively.

    Survival LLM: a free offline AI assistant and emergency library — 930,000 Wikipedia articles, zero internet after setup

    Survival LLM is a free, open-source Mac and Windows app that downloads a local model sized to your machine’s memory plus a cited emergency library — medical references, FEMA and CDC guidance, survival and repair manuals, iFixit guides and about 930,000 Wikipedia articles (9 GB full download, 1.3 GB without Wikipedia and iFixit). The honest part is the published test table: on 22 blind-graded blackout and first-aid questions, the top configurations (NVIDIA Nemotron, Qwen3.8 27B on 32 GB+ machines) got 21 of 22 main points right, while the 16–31 GB tier’s IBM Granite 4.2 8B got 14 of 22. Every answer lists its sources with the exact passage highlighted, precisely because the smaller models make more mistakes. Setup is one online session of 20 minutes to 2.5 hours, mostly downloading.

    • The design
      • Model scales with RAM: better hardware gets a bigger model; the app checks memory at first run and downloads what fits
      • Source-first answers: citations with highlighted passages are the mitigation for model error, stated plainly by the makers
    • Why it matters
      • Offline is a real requirement class — blackouts, disasters, dead zones — and this is a complete, testable attempt at it
      • The published 14/22 is the useful number: it tells you exactly how much to trust the small-model tier, which is “verify against the source”
    Source: brangus.io · Discuss: HN Discuss · 419 pts / 157 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread’s central argument is whether the LLM layer should exist at all. Critics say the same corpus behind plain search — Kiwix is the named example — is smaller, runs on weaker hardware and cannot hallucinate a first-aid step; one commenter describes building exactly that in 2018 for air-gapped schools: partial Wikipedia, an inverted index and TextRank summaries on a Raspberry Pi. The 14-of-22 figure draws the predictable joke about emergency appendectomies and a “just buy some books” verdict. Supporters like the direction — small, local, task-specific models instead of frontier everything-models — and offer llamafile and Project Nomad as alternatives, while a wishlist forms for cheaper endpoints: a finetuned small model on a $25 phone in an EMP-proof box with a solar charger. Nobody disputes the makers’ transparency about scores; the dispute is the architecture.

    Git for the US Code: statutes deserve blame and point-in-time lookup, not an afternoon in the amendment notes

    A Lexifina opinion piece (October 28) makes a tooling argument: a programmer can point at any line and see who wrote it, when, and what the file looked like on any past day; a lawyer reading a federal statute usually cannot. Outside the titles enacted as positive law, the Code is only prima facie evidence — the Statutes at Large govern — and the Office of the Law Revision Counsel publishes it as release points current through a named public law. Which law wrote a sentence lives in prose amendment notes; effective dates live in separate notes. The worked example is 11 USC § 1182(1): the small-business debt limit was set at $7.5 million by the CARES Act in March 2020, lapsed March 27, 2022, was restored that June retroactively over the gap, expired again in June 2024, and stands at $3,424,000 after the April 2025 adjustment. The AI angle is explicit: first-pass legal research now runs through language models that cannot use a law’s lineage.

    • The proposal
      • Version-control semantics for statutes: per-line provenance, effective-date tracking, point-in-time text
      • Motivation includes machine readers: LLMs doing legal research are “handcuffed” without lineage, per the author
    • Why it matters
      • The § 1182(1) example is the proof: four regimes in five years for one definition, recoverable today only from prose notes
      • Provenance is infrastructure — whoever maintains it shapes what both lawyers and models can answer
    Source: lexifina.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 341 pts / 175 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingThe thread splits on whether the thing exists. The top comment calls the title fraudulent — “you can’t git-clone this” — and others cannot find anywhere on the page to try a product, reading it as a concept essay wearing a product title. Prior art surfaces quickly: France’s Law Factory versioned bills in git with visualisations a decade ago. A linked lawyer’s take tempers the premise: git-style change tracking is less helpful in practice than it sounds, partly because basic change tracking already exists. Ownership is the other fault line — a private company should not be the system of record for law, with a profit motive to rewrite history; the counter-proposal is to lobby the Office of the Law Revision Counsel to maintain the repo itself, retroactively. The naming committee contributes “legitslate,” and several commenters confess they had the same idea ten years ago and never finished it.

    Ten days later, macOS developer tools still trust the compromised eMudhra certificates — and 27.2 undid the manual fix

    A follow-up report dated October 28: Apple was notified of the eMudhra compromise on October 17, and the developer tools Apple ships with macOS — git, curl, python3 and the rest that read the system trust store — still trust the compromised certificates. macOS 27.2, released October 26, did not fix it and reverted the workaround users had applied by hand, leaving them exposed again; meanwhile, the post notes, dozens of other projects, many volunteer-run, shipped fixes days ago. Impact is stated plainly: anyone on the network path can intercept HTTPS connections these tools make — git clones and pulls can deliver attacker-chosen code, pushes can be altered, tokens can be captured. Safari and apps on Apple’s networking framework are already protected; the vendor fix is to remove the affected roots from /etc/ssl/cert.pem, and the page provides test commands (a test git remote and two test hosts) that should fail with a certificate error on a protected machine.

    • The state
      • Notified Oct 17; still trusted Oct 28; macOS 27.2 (Oct 26) shipped without the fix and undid manual removals
      • Affected surface: tools using /etc/ssl/cert.pem — git, curl, openssl, python3 and peers; Safari-class apps already fixed
    • Why it matters
      • This is a supply-chain hole with a one-command test: if the demo clone succeeds, your git HTTPS path is interceptable
      • Update behaviour is part of the bug — an OS update silently restoring a removed bad root teaches users their fix did not stick
    Source: emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 336 pts / 137 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingFraming is the first fight: the title blames Git, commenters correct it — git is “just a victim,” the bug is the system CA store and applies to curl, openssl, Python, Perl and Ruby on macOS alike. Homebrew users check whether their builds dodge it (they use the same store unless built otherwise, is the working assumption in-thread), and several report running the test commands with mixed local results. Threat scale is the second fight: one side calls interception risk overblown because a MITM position is itself a big compromise; the other asks how a vendor that shipped an OS release mid-incident did not include a root removal. Commenters link Apple’s own support statement for the official line, and the practical advice that recurs is habit-forming: prefer SSH remotes, because “this is gonna keep happening.”

    Tailscale SSH investigation update: four bulletins, mostly privilege escalation inside the root-running daemon

    Tailscale’s investigation page, updated October 27, now points to four security bulletins and a findings post. TS-2026-014: Tailscale SSH’s Unix socket forwarding could grant peers access to root-only sockets — privileged dials via local forwarding, socket replacement via remote forwarding, and a race that defeated the path allowlist. TS-2026-015: unbounded memory growth in SSH session handling (window-change messages, environment appends) as a denial of service, fixed in 1.104.4. TS-2026-016: tailscale serve Unix backends could reach privileged sockets, including the tailscaled local API socket, dialed as root. TS-2026-017: an HTTP/2 shared serve context could bleed Tailscale-App-Capabilities values across mounts. The company’s own summary: most Tailscale SSH issues concern privilege escalation — someone already authorized to connect may be able to act beyond their configured local user’s permissions.

    • The pattern
      • Four findings, one shape: a root-privileged daemon doing per-user work — forwarding, proxying, session state — with checks done lexically, at config time, or in shared state
      • Action: review the bulletins and update; the DoS fix is in client 1.104.4 or newer
    • Why it matters
      • Tailscale SSH replaces key management with identity and ACLs — a privesc inside it breaks exactly the boundary the ACLs promise
      • Headscale users are affected too, per the thread: the vulnerable code is in tailscaled, not the control plane
    Source: tailscale.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 323 pts / 170 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingScope is the argument. A former Tailscale employee who reviewed the fixes narrows it: this bites where ACLs permit Tailscale SSH into a node without a sudo/root path — if you only SSH as yourself or to sudoers, little changes. Others push back that least-privilege teams are precisely the exposed ones, and quote a restricted backup user on a NAS as the canonical victim. The language gets read closely too — “most,” “in some circumstances” — with one camp calling it mid-investigation caution and another calling it weaselly. Two corrections recur: Tailscale SSH is opt-in, not shipped on by default, and the researcher write-up at tailworm.com is linked repeatedly as the more detailed source. The exit thread is predictable and split: some are moving homelabs to plain WireGuard or stock OpenSSH and accepting the key-management work; others answer that they never removed that attack surface, they just took ownership of it.

    Ink droplets in flight win Nikon Small World 2026: a deconstructed inkjet, a dark room, and a flash for a shutter

    Ars Technica reports the 2026 Nikon Small World photomicrography winner: Ole Bielfeldt, a 25-year-old experimental photographer from Germany, photographed ink droplets being deposited on paper by an inkjet printer — beating 1,802 other entries from 69 countries. The droplets move faster than a camera shutter, so he deconstructed the printer to move the paper under a stationary printhead and shot in a dark room, using the flash as the shutter to freeze the drops mid-air. No pricey equipment and no AI — traditional technique, per the report. Bielfeldt started during COVID lockdown with an old microscope found in his parents’ attic, a homemade tube-and-wood camera adaptor, and trial and error; he posts behind-the-scenes work as macrofying.

    • The shot
      • 20x magnification for the winning image; his earlier highly ranked candle and match photographs used 2.5x
      • Technique over gear: stationary head, moving paper, darkness, flash-as-shutter
    • Why it matters
      • A mundane machine becomes unrecognisable at droplet scale — the contest’s point, made without AI generation
      • Small thread, high delight: 51 comments and almost no argument, which on this front page is itself notable
    Source: arstechnica.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 304 pts / 51 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingDelight, mostly. Several commenters admit they first assumed the image was AI-generated and are pleased to be wrong; others call inkjet printers some of the most insane technology in ordinary life once you see what the printhead is actually doing. The runners-up get their share: the second-place jumping spider has declared fans (“just so adorable,” with sinister-looking pollen beside it), and third place — neurons in an expanded mouse brain — prompts a genuine “I didn’t know brains actually look like this inside.” Practical readers go looking for the rig: the photographer’s earlier contest entries were chemical-reaction shots of a candle and a match at 2.5x magnification, and a linked video shows the custom setup behind the ink shot.
