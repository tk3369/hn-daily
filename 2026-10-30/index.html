    Mistral Large 4's weights are out — 1T parameters, aimed at coding and cyber defence

    Mistral released the weights of Large 4 (Le Chonk), a 1-trillion-parameter multimodal model, after three more weeks of reinforcement learning since its preview. It claims the open-weight lead in coding and defensive cybersecurity, at $0.70 input / $2.10 output per million tokens.

    • What it claims
      • FrontierCode 2.0 lead at 19.7%; Terminal-Bench 4.0 up 13 points to 41% since preview.
      • Tied with GLM-5.3 at 48% on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, using 47% fewer tokens per task.
    • Tradeoffs
      • The licence is a modified MIT: Model-as-a-Service businesses need a separate commercial licence, so commenters dispute calling it fully open.
      • A 96.6% refusal rate on malicious cyber requests is claimed — but the thread asks about false refusals on legitimate defensive work.
    Source: mistral.ai · Discuss: HN Discuss · 1584 pts / 414 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingWelcome for a European sovereign open model dominates, with pricing halved from preview making a reassessment worthwhile. Debate centres on the modified-MIT licence, missing benchmark rivals such as Kimi, and whether refusal stats mean anything without false-refusal rates.

    An Arkham-labelled entity page titled Binance-hacker-1029 lists about $5.6B allegedly stolen from Binance. No confirmed Binance statement appears in the thread, so this is a circulating claim, not an established theft — treat the headline number as unverified.

    • What is circulating
      • On-chain posts cited in the thread say about $2.4B in USDT/USDC is already frozen by Circle and Tether.

    A backdoored Claude Code extension slipped into Cursor's marketplace

    Reports say a maliciously modified Claude Code VS Code extension appeared on the Cursor marketplace and Open VSX, and Cursor has published a security notice. Early thread discussion said the official Microsoft marketplace build was clean, but that was later marked uncertain — check your version and source before trusting it.

    • What happened
      • The suspect build reportedly delivered a backdoor to developer machines; one commenter links it to the Binance theft claim, unconfirmed.
      • Cursor's notice is the firmest source in the thread; the original post is a social-media report, not a vendor advisory.
    • What to do
      • Check which marketplace and version you installed, remove any suspect build, and audit for a reverse shell.
      • Some machines may have escaped by luck: new versions become eligible for auto-update only 12 hours after publication.
    Source: cyberplace.social · Discuss: HN Discuss · 531 pts / 156 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingShock at the distribution scale dominates, alongside a bleaker point: developers running coding CLIs daily already grant near-RCE trust, so 'don't curl | bash' rings hollow. Timing and version details are still being pinned down, with several users unsure whether they ever received the bad build.

    Martin Fowler: LLM prose has a voice, and it kills the urge to read

    Martin Fowler argues LLM-generated prose triggers an uncanny-valley reaction — low information density, familiar tics, nothing to gain from engaging — and that delegating writing means delegating thinking. His rule: write the prose yourself, use an LLM at most for an outline, and declare any AI help.

    • Core argument
      • Detectors miss the point: the reaction is human, and by the time you reach for a detector it is already too late.
      • Even a human-edited LLM draft is hard to purge of LLM voice; one known exception needed a professional editor at a major periodical.
    • Exceptions
      • Accessibility is a genuine exception — a contributor with Parkinson's disease relies on LLM help to keep writing.
      • Translation and form letters are awkward cases, but Fowler still prefers clunky human prose to smooth, soulless text.
    Source: martinfowler.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 523 pts / 279 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingMany readers recognise themselves adopting LLM style even without using one, simply from reading so much of it. The split is between accessibility and empowerment — LLM help lets some people communicate at all — and prose judgement punishing real technical insight; several expect indistinguishability within months to a couple of years.

    H.264 is magic — a 2016 explainer that still gets re-read yearly

    Sid Bala's 2016 classic walks through how 300 frames of video fit in 175KB while a single PNG frame takes 1,015KB: motion prediction, transforms and entropy coding, not a miracle. It resurfaces on HN every year or two, and this round is no exception.

    • The trick
      • Inter-frame prediction stores only what changes, then lossy transforms discard what eyes barely notice.
      • The PNG comparison is lossy versus lossless, so the 1,500x figure is illustrative, not a fair benchmark.
    • Why it resurfaces
      • Readers call re-reading it a tradition, alongside the 3Blue1Brown 'Compression is Intelligence' video.
      • The 2026 follow-up in the thread: AV1 adoption is the current magic, with AV2 on the way.
    Source: sidbala.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 474 pts / 155 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingNostalgia and practice mix: annual re-reads, a practical 'should I use H.264 or H.265?' thread, and a note that the article itself is dated 2016. Previous HN discussions in 2016, 2019, 2022 and 2024 each drew 180–219 comments.

    50 AI agents built a rationing bureaucracy in Minecraft

    YC Labs and Olam Labs ran 50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents for three hours in Minecraft with one goal — 'build a thriving civilization' — plus Mindustry swarms. The agents formed a hierarchy, a ration ledger, written rules and permits, and sent about 5,900 messages; 22 of 34 deaths came on the first night, and caution took over.

    • What emerged
      • Ada, first in the directory, became de facto leader; Ines ran the food queue from a shared ledger.
      • After the first-night deaths, agents huddled in lit shelters and abandoned ambitious tasks, rationing scarce food.
    • Limits
      • The play felt turn-based, and three hours with an open goal gave weak incentives for lively, reactive play.
      • The hierarchy may be an artefact of alphabetical directory order, not earned leadership — a confound the thread flags.
    Source: labs.ycombinator.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 335 pts / 134 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingDelight dominates — a three-minute 'no blame' inquiry over a stolen cooked chicken, agents as 'super-serious little senior engineers' — plus praise for the embedded gameplay widget. The serious question is cost: how much KV cache do 50 game agents use, and could they fit on one consumer GPU?

    The 'Anthropic hacked' post is really a Cursor marketplace extension scare

    A widely shared post claims Anthropic was hacked to deliver a remote-access trojan to Claude developer laptops worldwide. Cursor's own notice, cited in the thread, says something narrower: a maliciously modified Claude Code extension was made available in its marketplace. The headline overstates what is confirmed.

    • What Cursor confirmed, per the thread
      • A malicious version of the Claude Code extension appeared in the Cursor VS Code extension marketplace.
      • The trojan angle is an SSH reverse backdoor, per the post — a machine-level compromise, not just a data leak.
    • What remains thin
      • The original post is low on details, its social links are hard to follow, and scope and timing are still unclear.
      • Commenters correct the framing: Anthropic itself was not shown hacked; a marketplace distribution channel was abused.
    Source: doublepulsar.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 312 pts / 58 comments

    What HN commenters are saying'How legit is this? How worried should we be?' is the dominant question, from users whose organisations run the extension. One silver lining offered: if the motive was crypto theft, non-crypto machines may have lost nothing of value — but the reverse shell still has to be removed.

    elect.horse: gerrymandering, as a playable puzzle

    elect.horse is a browser puzzle about drawing electoral districts — horses versus donkeys — that makes gerrymandering concrete instead of abstract. It follows enclose.horse and draws comparisons to Gerrymandle, with a colour-accessibility complaint in the thread.

    • Why it works
      • District lines, not just votes, decide the outcome — a lesson several commenters say should be assigned in schools.
      • Short, replayable puzzles: one player reports finishing the day-8 expert level in about 40 seconds.
    • Controversy
      • Commenters accuse the new owners of reskinning Gerrymandle 1:1, down to odd UI choices, with ads and LLM-generated copy.
      • Others defend it as fun regardless of provenance; the horse/donkey colours are hard to distinguish for some players.
    Source: elect.horse · Discuss: HN Discuss · 293 pts / 79 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingPlayers call it fun and genuinely instructive, and the school-assignment suggestion recurs. The counter-thread is about ownership: enclose.horse was sold off, and the new version is seen as a shameless cash grab rather than an improvement.

    Herdr ships a native app for running coding agents in terminals

    Herdr, a terminal multiplexer built for coding-agent sessions, has released a native app. It uses libghostty under the hood, avoiding a terminal-inside-a-terminal, and a GUI variant, herdr-gpui, adds remote phone pairing plus browser integration for sending screenshots and notes to agents.

    • Why users switch
      • Long-time tmux users in the thread say they switched this year and have not looked back, citing agent integrations.
      • Sessions stay rock-solid across multiple linked machines, with phone check-ins via a companion app.
    • Open questions
      • Monetisation for VC-backed multiplexers is unanswered, and comparisons to CMUX and Mjolnir recur.
      • One reader reports Chrome flagging herdr.dev's SSL certificate as invalid and is waiting before trying it.
    Source: github.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 272 pts / 181 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingEnthusiasm from existing users dominates, including a Windows 98 mode with Minesweeper, Solitaire and Clippy. Sceptics ask what the business model is, and how it compares with CMUX and the ACP-based Mjolnir, which offers cross-provider subagents and session-log sync.

    A tiny world model plays games on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W

    One tiny world model learns the dynamics of several games and runs on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W, a microcontroller, not a GPU box. Looped transformers were needed for mini-Doom but not for the simpler games, according to the thread.

    • What impresses
      • The same model covers multiple games, with a determinism commenters did not expect from neural nets.
      • The Pico framing cuts both ways: it proves efficiency, but some say it distracts from the world-model capability itself.
    • Questions
      • Training hardware is not in the readme, one commenter notes, and the actor's training on world-model mistakes is open.
      • If the actor learns from the model's errors, it could learn to force hallucinations into easier game states.
    Source: github.com · Discuss: HN Discuss · 243 pts / 16 comments

    What HN commenters are sayingReactions are near-uniform delight — 'incredibly cool', 'fascinating' — with a WebGPU port question and a broader claim: small, locally run models like this are the real long-term future of AI, not ever-larger cloud models.
