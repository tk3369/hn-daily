1

Astra edits model weights directly — and it works

OpenAI's Astra reportedly inspected and edited neural network weights directly — identifying what small networks were trained on and improving a small Qwen model's math performance several-fold, without conventional training tooling. If it generalizes, weight-space work could shortcut how models are tuned.

2875 pts · 867 comments | Source | HN Discuss
What HN commenters are saying

Consensus is genuine surprise — most commenters say they would not have predicted a model could reason about raw weights at all, and several frame it as a possible fast lane to recursive self-improvement. The pushback is about scale: the striking demos run on tiny networks, and skeptics argue tooling-assisted edits on small models are a long way from rewriting a frontier model.

Subvocal: talk to your computer without a sound

Glasses-mounted radar reads silent mouth movements and turns them into text and commands, so you can talk to your computer in a library, plane, or open office without making a sound. Open-source alternatives exist but are less polished.

773 pts · 360 comments | Source | HN Discuss
What HN commenters are saying

Enthusiasm for quiet computing in shared spaces, with sign language and throat mics raised as alternatives; the recurring objection is social awkwardness — mouthing words silently in public feels stranger than whispering — plus questions about why glasses are required and whether a throat mic would be simpler. Some dismiss it as over-engineering when typing is already fast.
