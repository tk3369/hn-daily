Postman crosses a line: silent AI plugins, API data at stake

A GitHub issue reports Postman silently auto-installing AI plugins and attempting to share all API data. For a tool that routinely holds production keys and internal endpoints, that is not a cosmetic annoyance — it is a trust break. The pragmatic move is to audit what is installed and shared, and keep secrets out of any client that can phone home.

Source HN Discuss 1409 pts · 464 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Commenters largely agree Postman has drifted from lightweight tool to bloated upsell machine, and swap recommendations for Bruno, Yaak and older Insomnia. The split is over monetisation itself: some argue developers deserve paid tools, others say account and cloud pressure is now inevitable in this category.

Jailbreaking a Renault nav unit just to fix the clock

An owner reverse-engineered a Renault navigation system because changing the time required a proprietary, easily lost SD card. It is a small fix exposing a larger design failure: a basic setting locked behind vendor-controlled hardware. The lesson is boring and useful — if users cannot set the clock, the system is not finished.

Source HN Discuss 890 pts · 334 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread turns into a catalogue of head-unit grievances, from Honda Bluetooth auto-play to VCR-clock nostalgia, plus warnings that un-updated DST rules will bite again in spring, especially in Canadian provinces dropping clock changes. Some say just fit an aftermarket unit; others want full custom firmware.

Stringman: an $800 cable robot that grips a whole room

Stringman is an open-source, room-scale gripper robot built around cables and tension rather than a rigid arm. At $800 it is cheap for the category and deliberately simple in concept. The open question is practical range: what it can reliably lift, reach and do in a real room, not just a demo.

Source HN Discuss 694 pts · 212 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Commenters praise the ingenuity and the “tension is all you need” idea, then immediately joke about strangulation and dropped objects. A longer split follows over robot form: humanoid generalists for human-shaped rooms versus cheaper, specialised designs that do one job well.

4

A laptop model that can read other models’ weights

Simon Willison reports Qwen 3.8 27B running on a laptop interpreting model weights, echoing the Astra work on reading and editing weights. If small open models can inspect weights usefully, interpretability stops being a frontier-only exercise. The catch is scope: reading a pattern is not the same as safely improving a model.

Source HN Discuss 682 pts · 197 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Commenters ask how far this scales — to Qwen 3.5 0.8B, or to its own weights — and push back on hype, noting single-task gains, looping fixes and abliteration-style tweaks are narrower than “self-improvement.” Others see a path to fixing heavily quantised models that loop.

5

Fujifilm bets 5x on tape as AI archiving explodes

Fujifilm plans to boost tape production fivefold as AI-driven data growth fuels archiving demand. Tape’s pitch is simple: cheap, dense, offline and air-gappable in a ransomware era. The risk is reading too much into one multiplier — capacity, drive prices and restore workflows decide whether archives are actually usable.

Source HN Discuss 509 pts · 216 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Commenters are sceptical that AI alone explains a 5x jump, pointing to never-deleted HPC data, harvest-now-decrypt-later fears and war-driven cold-storage demand. The sharper consensus is operational: many organisations have backups but few test restores, and LTO drive costs and shrinking backwards compatibility complicate the plan.

Safari 27.3 will dark-mode sites that never asked for it

Safari introduces Adaptive Dark Mode in 27.3, automatically transforming light-only pages for dark-mode users while shielding the transformation from site JavaScript. That is a user win and a developer headache in one feature: fewer blinding white pages, but less control over how a design actually renders.

Source HN Discuss 472 pts · 246 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Users mostly cheer, designers welcome the push to support prefers-color-scheme properly, and developers split: some call it a nightmare for pixel control, others say the web was never a fixed canvas. A privacy thread defends the browser “lying” to sites about transformed colours as fingerprinting protection.

8

C++26 fails approval — and the fight is really about contracts

C++26 has been disapproved at the final ballot stage, with the dispute centred on contracts — pre- and post-conditions for functions, pitched as better assertions. A failed standard is rare and costly: it delays the whole release and exposes a committee struggling to agree on scope, safety and process.

Source HN Discuss 371 pts · 271 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Commenters explain contracts as improved assertions, then split sharply: supporters see a readable safety and performance tool, critics call the proposal poorly specified and pushed despite unresolved concerns. A wider thread laments C++ complexity, with some arguing memory-safety pieces should be salvaged without the full contracts package.

9

AI is surfacing 40 years of technical debt, one vuln at a time

IEEE Spectrum’s php2shell case argues AI-assisted discovery is forcing payment on decades of accumulated technical debt in old, widely used code. Finding bugs faster does not fix the underlying problem — sprawling, rarely reviewed code paths in critical software. The work now is triage, simplification and patching, not just detection.

Source HN Discuss 355 pts · 244 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Commenters split between “return to KISS” — software that does less has less attack surface — and scepticism that KISS alone explains or fixes AI-found bugs. A parallel debate revives memory-safe languages, with some blaming NSA vulnerability stockpiling and others noting rewrites introduce new bugs and Ada’s mandated history.

Source HN Discuss 339 pts · 88 comments
What HN commenters are saying

A larger argument breaks out over whether a vibe-coded entry fits a human-creativity challenge, with some calling it AI slop and others defending LLM-assisted craft.
