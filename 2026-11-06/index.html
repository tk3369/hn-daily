Sshole: a 14-year-old musl quirk lets any long password into locked SSH accounts

A chain of small decisions — musl's crypt() returning '*' on failure, OpenSSH borrowing another account's salt for '*' accounts — means any 257–1024 character password logs into a locked account on musl systems without PAM. Alpine and OpenWrt are exposed; OpenSSH 10.6p2 fixes it.

Source HN Discuss 2823 pts · 688 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The name jokes continue, but the live debate is exposure: what actually runs musl without PAM plus an sshd with password auth? Some point to Docker containers running sshd at million-download scale; others say almost nobody runs sshd in a container, so real-world exposure is small. Remediation is agreed: set PasswordAuthentication=no or update to OpenSSH 10.6p2.

Claude Code sends your email address to websites

Claude Code adds a User-Agent header like session-slug/1 <name@example.com> when fetching the web, leaking paying customers' email addresses to every site it visits. The reporter says it brought dozens of scam and phishing emails a day from sites never personally visited.

Source HN Discuss 1369 pts · 433 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Outrage is the thread's register: a human engineer would never design this, say commenters, and the debate is whether a human or the model chose it — and whether it's anti-abuse or promotion. Nobody defends it as sensible; the fix people want is simply to stop sending the address.

MS Paint has a remote code execution vulnerability

CVE-2026-83844 is an out-of-bounds read in Windows Paint that, per the CVE record, lets an unauthenticated attacker execute code over the network with no user interaction required. Yes, Paint — the app that now ships with Copilot features.

Source HN Discuss 625 pts · 238 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Disbelief first, then confusion over the mechanism: Paint isn't a network service, so how is it remotely reachable? Theories range from a Copilot service talking to Paint over IPC to a command-line print path. The jokes write themselves — Minesweeper is next.

I wish tech companies knew more about me

The author's contrarian take: ads feel bad not because companies know too much, but because they know too little — a better profile would mean ads for things you actually want. It's a provocation, and the thread treats it as one.

Source HN Discuss 505 pts · 392 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The pushback is structural: advertisers pay for placement, so relevance is priced into the auction and the heaviest spenders are often the brands you least want. Several argue targeted advertising will never feel good because the seller's goal is to move their product, not to serve you.

Dutch 'idealist' bank ASN blocks non-Google Android, including GrapheneOS

ASN Bank — which markets itself on ideals — now blocks its app on non-Google Android, including GrapheneOS, via Play Integrity checks. For a bank whose customers chose it on principle, the thread reads it as a betrayal.

Source HN Discuss 405 pts · 239 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Other European banks do the same, note commenters — N26 blocks new signups on GrapheneOS — and workarounds are a second phone, or Bunq plus CurvePay for tap-to-pay. The bigger worry is Wero, the European payment system rolling out tap-to-pay in 2027, which will lean on the same checks.

Britain to make salary disclosure in job ads mandatory

Britain will require employers to publish salaries in job ads, PM Andy Burnham announced, to help young people applying for first jobs and close pay gaps that disadvantage women. Legislation is coming to parliament, with no timeline given.

Source HN Discuss 401 pts · 330 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread argues the edge cases — roles reshaped around a candidate, bait-and-switch gaming — against the core point, which is information asymmetry: knowing the pay before spending time applying changes the power balance. Most agree negotiation survives; the question is how much gaming the law invites.

10

The Hackintosh Reborn — with AI

AI-assisted reverse engineering — Claude Code plus Ghidra — has revived the Hackintosh scene: macOS 27 now runs on Snapdragon laptops, ARM single-board computers, even iOS 17 on a Switch OLED, with 50-series Nvidia GPU support in progress. The author built a working iOS app in a day on a ThinkPad running macOS 26 without writing a line of code.

Source HN Discuss 360 pts · 201 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Nostalgia and scepticism in equal measure — the old scene died on fragility (one update from breaking), and several say macOS on non-Apple hardware only ever made sense for headless build machines or tinkering. Others note the real story is ARM: Switch games now run natively on Macs, and the x86 era is ending.
