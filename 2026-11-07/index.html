Tesla winds down Cybertruck production after years of poor sales

Tesla is ending Cybertruck production after years of weak sales, per NBC News. The angular truck never found a mass market beyond early adopters, and the thread's verdict is blunt: a niche product with a niche audience.

Source HN Discuss 1075 pts · 1929 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread drifts from the truck itself to its battery: owners describe powering homes through multi-day outages after Hurricane Helene, and the debate is whether that's a real selling point or a niche case — other EVs do vehicle-to-home too, and a generator covers most outages. A side thread turns to brand toxicity, with several saying they'd skip Tesla solar and Powerwall on principle.

Libghostty 1.0: the terminal engine behind Ghostty goes stable

Mitchell Hashimoto's libghostty hits 1.0 — a C library that lets any app embed a full terminal emulator, with a stable ABI designed for future additions. Ghostty is the demo; herdr, cmux, JetBrains' embedded terminal and Ghostel for Emacs already build on it.

Source HN Discuss 821 pts · 133 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The big confusion is Ghostty vs Rex — commenters settle on a Chromium/Chrome analogy, with Rex as the paid product adding servers and agent integration. Others praise the stable-ABI design, compare libghostty to libvte, and note you can now build a terminal in ~1,500 lines of C.

A small model that asks Opus for help matches Opus at 4x lower cost

Hightouch's post shows a small model orchestrating identity-resolution work and escalating only the hard calls to Opus, matching Opus accuracy at a quarter of the cost. The pattern: cheap model does the bulk, frontier model handles the edge cases.

Source HN Discuss 527 pts · 125 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Many run the same pattern already — a local Qwen3 27B as orchestrator or worker, Opus as planner or escalation target — and the debate is direction: big-to-small vs small-to-big. Consensus is it's task-dependent: the smartest model needs either the full context or a crisp problem definition, not both.

FAA to phase out floppy disks from air traffic control by 2028

The FAA says it will retire floppy disks from air-traffic-control systems by 2028. The disks have outlived every expectation, and the announcement lands as both overdue modernization and a reminder of how slowly safety-critical systems change.

Source HN Discuss 365 pts · 298 comments
What HN commenters are saying

A former FAA modernization engineer says the safety process itself is the bottleneck — rigorous, but a blocker to reasonable change for 15+ years. The thread then argues floppy reliability at length: data-recovery anecdotes of high failure rates vs. disks that still read after 30 years on a shelf.

Need for Speed Underground 2 now runs in the browser

The 2004 classic is playable in a browser tab via WebGPU and WebAssembly — no install, just click and play. It runs at 60fps even on a 5-year-old Android phone, which says as much about modern web bloat as about the port.

Source HN Discuss 304 pts · 89 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Pure nostalgia — the soundtrack, the car customization, the first race everyone remembers. The 'no download' claim gets pushback (500MB+ lands in the browser cache), there's an EA takedown worry, no Steam Deck controller support yet, and requests for Underground 1 and Hot Pursuit 2.

Every Mac ships an undocumented family-member classifier

A researcher found macOS daemons since 2022 build a local graph of who you know and how you're related — a 'personal knowledge graph' feeding Apple Intelligence. It runs even with Siri and Apple Intelligence switched off, and there's no documented way to disable it.

Source HN Discuss 299 pts · 146 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Split between 'creepy, can't opt out, a GDPR problem' and 'it's local, that's the point.' Many who ran the author's iKnow inspector report the classifier is wildly inaccurate — fake contacts labelled family, real partners missing. A long sub-thread criticizes the article itself as AI-written, which the author concedes.

Firecracker VM escape: PwnEscape part 2

Researchers demonstrate a VM escape from Firecracker, via a bug in the Linux kernel's networking code. The chain gets code execution out of the guest — the second Firecracker escape in days, and cloud providers reportedly panic-bounced fleets.

Source HN Discuss 260 pts · 110 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Heated debate over whether it's a Firecracker bug or a kernel bug: the demo ran without the jailer, so a properly jailed deployment limits the blast radius — but tptacek argues code execution behind the jailer is still effectively game over. Others pivot to microhypervisors and formal verification.

First steps in bare-metal programming using UEFI

Source HN Discuss 234 pts · 48 comments
