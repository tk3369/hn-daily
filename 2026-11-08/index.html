Need for Speed Underground 2 now runs in the browser

The 2004 classic is playable in a browser tab via WebGPU and WebAssembly — no install, just click and play, with free-roam multiplayer. Twenty years after EA shut down online play, the community brought it back to the browser.

Source HN Discuss 1052 pts · 265 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Nostalgia dominates — the soundtrack, the car customization, the first race everyone remembers. But the legality question keeps surfacing: the site hosts game assets, so an EA takedown is a real worry. Players also report stutters from asset loading, no manual shifting, and no controller support yet.

Every Mac ships an undocumented family-member classifier

A researcher found macOS daemons since 2022 build a local graph of who you know and how you're related — a 'personal knowledge graph' feeding Apple Intelligence. It runs even with Siri and Apple Intelligence switched off, and there's no documented way to disable it. The author joined the thread to answer questions.

Source HN Discuss 773 pts · 420 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Split between 'creepy, can't opt out, a GDPR problem' and 'it's local, that's the point.' Several who poked around report it still runs with all intelligence features off — one says it may push them to Debian. Others ask whether Little Snitch can block the daemons, and whether iPhones do the same.

Claude Code's “Don't Ask Again” exposes passwords in 1,122 public GitHub files

Apiiro scanned GitHub and found 1,122 public files where Claude Code's approved-command lists contain passwords and secrets — committed .claude/settings.local.json files. The nuance matters: 'Don't Ask Again' alone doesn't leak; committing the local settings file does.

Source HN Discuss 470 pts · 330 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The headline gets called confusing — the files sit in repos, and whether they're public depends on repo settings. Boris from the Claude Code team replies that settings.local.json is meant to stay local and is auto-gitignored when Claude creates it, and says the team is looking at secret scrubbing.

Every Windows 11 app should kill Electron, and Discord just showed why

Discord's native Windows rewrite reportedly drops the startup screen and cuts RAM ~70% even with two accounts signed in — the latest exhibit in the case against Electron. The numbers are pre-production, which the thread is quick to note.

Source HN Discuss 379 pts · 378 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Electron's value prop is one team shipping cross-platform with minimal effort — the article doesn't address that. Others hope for a native-app wave via Rust/GPUI, while a counter-thread notes Electron's real advantages: accessibility and non-English IME support that most Rust GUI toolkits still lack.

MaxxBench: “Nice DeepSWE score. But is it true?”

MaxxBench mutates DeepSWE tasks slightly and finds smaller models collapse hardest — raising the question of whether high benchmark scores reflect real capability or benchmark overfitting. The trend line is the story: the smaller the model, the bigger the drop.

Source HN Discuss 342 pts · 95 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Chronology argues against deliberate benchmaxxing — DeepSWE shipped in May, DeepSeek v4 Flash in April — but the core question stands: why do minor task changes cause catastrophic drops? The thread's summary: 'the good models are good and the benchmaxxed models are benchmaxxed.'

Show HN: Coldframe, a honeypot that poses as a supermarket fridge controller

A honeypot that pretends to be a supermarket refrigeration controller — default logins open a fake store dashboard with defrost buttons, so researchers see attackers' second move, not just the first probe. It follows real supermarket fridge hacks.

Source HN Discuss 258 pts · 61 comments
