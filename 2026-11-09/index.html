Goldman Sachs pulls Claude Code from bankers and traders

Goldman is restricting Claude Code for its traders and bankers, per Bloomberg — agent-written code from people who can't review it is a bad fit for a regulated trading floor. Tools bankers built get handed to the firm's ~12,000 engineers to harden instead.

Source HN Discuss 832 pts · 448 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The sourcing draws a flag first: the linked site looks AI-written, and the original is Bloomberg. On the policy itself, consensus is that it's sensible for a regulated business — unreviewed code on a trading floor, plus information-barrier problems. The counter: design safer abstractions for non-technical users instead of banning the tool, and note Barclays is moving the other way. Skeptics doubt 'improved' by engineers means much — more likely rewritten, or left in the backlog.

Declarative Dragging: specify where a drag can go, not how

UIST 2026 Best Paper: instead of hand-coding drag geometry, feedback, and animation, you declare which future states dragging an element can lead to — the system renders those possible worlds and blends them into a smooth interaction. Interactive demos included.

Source HN Discuss 644 pts · 81 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Delight at the demos, which commenters call satisfying and genuinely educational. The paper link circulates, and one thread calls it timely for agentic coding: LLMs can't QA the 'feel' of motion, so a provably-correct foundation removes the need. Some ask about prior art, pointing at the Hazel programming environment.

Outlook.com now rejects mail from domains without DMARC

Since November 5, Microsoft's consumer mail has quietly bounced some mail from domains with no DMARC record — even with valid SPF and DKIM. No announcement; the fix is one DNS record. If you email outlook.com or hotmail.com users, check yours.

Source HN Discuss 472 pts · 325 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Broad agreement it's the right direction — phishing risk outweighs the burden — but the unannounced weekend rollout drew fire from people mitigating it for hundreds of customers. The debate: is DMARC now just the cost of doing business, or an undue burden on small self-hosted operators?
