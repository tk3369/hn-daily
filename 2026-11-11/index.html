Smart home devices should work without the internet

Ten years of home-automation rules from the author: nothing core to the house should depend on a vendor's server. The setup is Tasmota, MQTT and Node-RED on a Pi — lights and switches keep working when the Wi-Fi or the cloud goes down.

Source HN Discuss 1100 pts · 394 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Applause for the rules — physical switches must survive automation, 'no network, no problem' — but skepticism too: 99% of products ship cloud-first, so the fix isn't a weekend hack. A side debate over the post's AI-polished prose vs the author's rougher first draft, which he promised to clean up.

Scion is back, selling a kei truck in America

Toyota is reviving Scion to sell a small 4WD kei truck in the US — but only to fleets, capped at 25 mph to meet low-speed-vehicle rules. Farmers, campuses and job sites are the pitch; it's a road-legal utility cart, not a daily driver.

Source HN Discuss 518 pts · 347 comments

Target-market debate: farmers, universities and groundskeeping fleets, plus retirement communities where LSVs already rule the golf-cart paths. $25k feels steep next to used imports, and others note UTVs and golf carts already fill this niche in rural towns.

Synaptics patent claims stop work on DisplayLink drivers

Synaptics sent a cease-and-desist to the developer of Vino, an open-source DisplayLink driver, claiming patent infringement — and work on the driver has stopped. The letter demanded compliance within five days, and the cited patents may only go up to claim 10.

Source HN Discuss 441 pts · 254 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Anger at the five-day deadline and the lawyer's name (Bateman). One commenter argues the patent likely falls — the claims were narrowed during prosecution to get granted. Others note DisplayLink was acquired by Synaptics in 2022, and the license model is the real revenue.
