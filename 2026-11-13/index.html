Rust 1.100 stabilizes the never type and custom allocators

Rust 1.100 is out, headlined by the long-awaited stabilization of the never type (!) and custom allocators, plus dependency cooldowns — Cargo can now ignore crate versions published too recently — and cargo install reusing the existing lockfile. A milestone release after years of work on these features.

Source HN Discuss 786 pts · 286 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Celebration, mostly — ‘finally, never happened’ — with one commenter still yearning for a stable ABI. Another just fixed arena leaks with allocator-api2 and can now drop the dependency.

Show HN: a $300 embroidery machine demanded a subscription, so he built his own app

A $300 embroidery machine needed a paid subscription to run, so its owner built Zigzag, an open-source app that talks to the machine over Bluetooth instead. A small, concrete win against subscription-locked hardware — and a demo of what one person plus an LLM can now ship.

Source HN Discuss 632 pts · 211 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Praise for the DIY spirit and the LLM-empowered angle. Practical questions follow — which machines are supported, is there a standard protocol? — and a broader call to kill hardware subscriptions app by app until vendors sell hardware with no strings attached.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen and Orchestrator: Google’s two-model coding loop

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Antigravity with Orchestrator, which hands work back and forth between models in one trajectory: Carbon explores, plans and resolves ambiguity read-only, then Hydrogen writes the edits, updates packages and runs tests — switching back when it hits friction.

Source HN Discuss 458 pts · 142 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Fans like not switching models manually; skeptics are nervous about delegating model choice to a model, which tends to be optimistic about its own capabilities. Others compare it to Anthropic’s advisor mode and GitHub’s multi-model pipeline — and mock the name scheme.

A new dwarf planet candidate, 2026 ED138, takes ~280 years to orbit the Sun

Astronomers found a possible new dwarf planet far beyond Pluto — 2026 ED138, on a 280-year orbit tilted nearly 25 degrees. It was spotted in modern survey data and traced back to photographic plates from 1937. Confirmation would add another member to the family Pluto joined in 2006.

Source HN Discuss 353 pts · 151 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Excitement at finding it in 1937 plates, and ‘is this Planet X?’ The Pluto debate resurfaces too: with Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake, several say the dwarf-planet class was the right call all along.
