FAA ground-stops all US departures for 4+ hours, then lifts it

Routers overloaded with data knocked out radar and comms at FAA centers Friday. Stop ran 3:54–8:15pm ET, departures only — airborne planes stayed up. 7,000+ flights canceled, 300k–400k stranded. DHS: no sign of a cyberattack.

Source HN Discuss 1598 pts · 1213 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Consensus: the vague “extraordinary circumstances” wording frustrated everyone, and the fix — locating the offending system and taking it offline — reads as overdue. Disagreement: whether this was a one-off router failure or proof the decades-old ATC system needs the $50B overhaul Duffy is pushing; several tracked the chaos live on flight trackers.

Nvidia allegedly funded AI startups to buy its own GPUs

A WSJ report alleges Nvidia invested in AI startups and small cloud providers that then bought its GPUs, propping up demand while moving a TSMC chip oversupply. CFO Kress reportedly pushed to cut the circular deals.

Source HN Discuss 823 pts · 492 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Many say this was already public a year ago — the Guardian covered circular financing in Dec 2025 — so the news is the scale and the internal fight. Disagreement: whether it’s smart business (deploying capital into high-growth equity) or demand manipulation; gamers just hope GPU prices finally fall.

If AI solved coding, why is my software still the same?

Jonathan Blow’s argument: AI gives you more of what you already optimize for, faster — but orgs haven’t changed what they optimize or how they work, so the software feels identical. Coding was never the bottleneck; culture and goals were.

Source HN Discuss 385 pts · 541 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Consensus: the bottleneck is organizational, not technical — “a thousand times this.” Disagreement: some report dramatically better personal software and astounding individual output, while others say teammates actively resist the tools and nothing changes.

Why buy an expensive Pi when an old phone will do?

An old phone has the CPU, RAM, storage, Wi-Fi and battery a Pi project needs — for free, from a drawer. The case for repurposing instead of buying new, with the caveats: no GPIO header, and Android is harder to tame than Raspberry Pi OS.

Source HN Discuss 251 pts · 159 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Consensus: for software-only homelab work, a phone wins; the Pi’s edge is GPIO and a tinker-friendly dev experience. The open question is supply — finding hundreds of matching phones with healthy batteries — plus a safety tip: pull the battery and use an emulator circuit.
