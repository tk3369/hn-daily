Optimizely compromise served malware on Zoom and GitLab

A compromise at Optimizely let attackers push malicious code through its experimentation platform onto major sites including Zoom and GitLab. Client-side A/B testing hands a third party arbitrary code execution on your pages — one compromised account becomes a massive incident.

Source HN Discuss 492 pts · 200 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Consensus: third-party tags are the root problem — marketing adds 30 trackers via Google Tag Manager and a Content Security Policy becomes unmanageable. The counter: server-side A/B testing avoids this entirely. Others say vet everything, self-host analytics, and cut down to two or three services.

Jiff 1.0 — a datetime library for Rust, from the author of ripgrep

Jiff 1.0 is out: a new Rust datetime library from BurntSushi, designed as the successor to chrono with a cleaner API — separate date, time, and datetime types and sane timezone handling. The chrono maintainer shows up in the thread.

Source HN Discuss 413 pts · 88 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The former chrono maintainer explains he quit from burnout after years of resistance to API improvements; the chrono author thanks him for his service. Consensus: datetime APIs are brutally hard — daylight saving is political — and Jiff's separate date/time types fix real modelling mistakes that Arrow and Moment make.

Microsoft blames its monster email outage on a defect from a July fix

Microsoft says the huge email outage traces to a software defect introduced in a July fix — and restoring service was a slow, chaotic 'black start' across hundreds of teams. The thread's engineers are unsurprised: big outages always look like this.

Source HN Discuss 388 pts · 293 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The black-start analogy sparks a long engineering digression — power-grid restarts, motor constants, solar inverters. Insiders say there is no such thing as a clean Microsoft black start: build-outs are human-driven, tribal-knowledge affairs taking weeks. Skeptics ask why cold-starting services was never planned for.

OpenCode Browse — the agent harness now browses the web for you

Source HN Discuss 311 pts · 191 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread is really about sandboxing: one camp says security belongs outside the harness — a sandbox, not permission popups, since permission fatigue is bad UX. The other says most users never configure a sandbox, so harnesses marketed to average users must ship strict-by-default permissions. Both agree sandboxing should be default-on and composable.
