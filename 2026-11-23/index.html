Jensen Huang is Nvidia CEO again — board votes unanimously to reinstate him

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately, 11 days after his November 12 resignation. The board says it is satisfied with his candor and ability to lead, and that input from senior US government levels gives confidence the move poses no national security risk. The independent committee's investigation continues.

Spelunkr: a free TV network built from the Internet Archive's forgotten videos

Spelunkr.tv turns the Internet Archive's public-domain video trove into scheduled TV channels — old anime, black-and-white horror and other forgotten footage, playing on a broadcast schedule instead of an on-demand feed. No account, no ads: you tune in and watch what's on. The charm is the constraint — television the way it used to work.

Source HN Discuss 703 pts · 132 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Delight, mostly. People report finding Macross on the old anime channel and Vincent Price horror on another, and several call it a cool anachronism — internet video without the algorithm. The requests are practical: show descriptions for upcoming programs, and explain how it was built.

3

OpenAI: perplexity is the metric that matters — and it should pace AI development

OpenAI argues pretraining perplexity — how well a model predicts the next token — is the single metric tracking the emergence of new capabilities, from GPT-2's coherent paragraphs to reasoning at GPT-4. It publishes its internal numbers (GPT-3.5 ~0.99, GPT-4 ~0.91, now below 0.8) and proposes shared industry limits: don't cross a perplexity threshold until safety monitoring catches up.

Source HN Discuss 639 pts · 417 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread mostly plays the game itself: humans are terrible at next-token prediction, and beating even a small LLM at it is basically impossible — one commenter links the paper showing exactly that. The serious debate is whether perplexity can be a shared yardstick at all, given different labs use different datasets, and that chain-of-thought gains don't show up in perplexity.

GLM-5.4: a Loop Transformer that reasons deeper without more parameters

Z.ai's GLM-5.4 is its first flagship on the Loop Transformer: 28 layers of the 750B base model run twice per pass, reusing weights, so it reasons more deeply at the same parameter count — claimed comparable to 2–3T-parameter models. It ships with higher subscription limits and up to 200 tok/s off-peak. The catch: the post publishes no benchmark scores.

Source HN Discuss 514 pts · 200 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The missing benchmarks are the whole thread. Either the scores aren't impressive or they'd have included them — or they're being held for a second news cycle. Others argue benchmarks have become marketing anyway, and one suspects the cyber-safety hardening will lower scores later, so the drop is better left unquantified.

7

XNU Linux: the full macOS desktop running as a kernel module inside Linux

XNU Linux builds Apple's open-source XNU kernel as a loadable module (xnu.ko) inside an unmodified arm64 Linux kernel, booting a real macOS 27 system disk to the desktop — Finder, Dock, Safari — next to Linux processes on the same CPUs, no hypervisor. It boots in 31–48 seconds on Apple Silicon VMs and runs bare-metal on a DGX Spark.

Source HN Discuss 465 pts · 199 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Awe, plus the legal question: will Apple object? Most say no — it's built from Apple's published sources, and Apple never went after Hackintosh hobbyists, only resellers. The practical question is ‘cool, but why?’ — answered, for most of the thread, by the demo itself: a macOS desktop and a Linux shell side by side.

A miniature San Francisco with live buses, planes and boats

SmallSF is a live miniature of San Francisco — buses, planes and boats moving in real time, fed by GTFS real-time transit data and ADS-B flight tracking, rendered as a tiny city you can zoom into. It's a demo with a real engineering question underneath: how do you interpolate between sparse position updates so motion stays smooth at street zoom?

Source HN Discuss 314 pts · 101 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The top question is exactly that engineering one — client-side dead reckoning or a fixed tick buffer between GTFS-RT bus updates and ADS-B plane feeds? Plus the observation that car traffic is janky, and a debate over whether pixel-art miniatures are ‘empty slop’ or whether that's precisely the point of a miniature.

A medieval cathedral floor emerges in Sudan — one of the biggest ever found

Archaeologists in Old Dongola, northern Sudan, uncovered the original floor of the Great Church of Jesus, a medieval cathedral estimated at 47 by 57 metres — larger than the White House, and possibly the largest church in medieval Nubia. The Polish-Sudanese team reached it via an 11-metre-high staircase; the church was first found in 2021 but most of it is still buried.

Source HN Discuss 304 pts · 104 comments
What HN commenters are saying

History buffs take over — comparisons to Hagia Sophia, Notre-Dame and Córdoba, with corrections flying: Notre-Dame started in 1163, Córdoba wasn't conquered until 1236, the Armada was 1588. The deeper debate is whether pointed arches came from Syria after the First Crusade or evolved independently in several places at once.
