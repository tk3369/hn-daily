Matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4 — OpenAI posts the proof

OpenAI's math team posted a preprint proving the matrix multiplication exponent ω is exactly 2.25, down from the prior ~2.371 bound — the first exact value rather than a better upper bound. It's one of nine results in the repo. No practical GPU kernel yet, but the techniques behind the bound may eventually matter for large-scale linear algebra.

Source HN Discuss 814 pts · 452 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread's first job was fixing the title: the result is Θ(n^2.25) — exactly 2.25, not an O() upper bound. Commenters note it improves the prior ~2.371 bound and ask whether it extends to rectangular matrices. The practical question dominates the rest: can the techniques behind the bound become real GPU kernels? The economic implications, one commenter says, would be enormous.

Button Sounds: a library of professionally recorded clicks for your UI

Josh Comeau recorded a collection of button clicks and presses in a sound-treated room and put them online to preview and use in your own interfaces. The right click sound makes a UI feel physical — small detail, big difference. Desktop works best: iOS Safari blocks audio unless silent mode is off.

Source HN Discuss 602 pts · 95 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Delight, mostly — ‘extremely satisfying’ and ‘a total joy to play around with.’ The iOS Safari audio quirk gets a fix in the thread: setting navigator.audioSession.type to ‘playback’ lets sounds play even in silent mode. One commenter asks whether the sounds are MIT-licensed; another wants a writeup of the recording process itself.

Ask HN: Anthropic accused my company of distilling — how do I prove we're not?

A founder says Anthropic accused their company of distilling its models and cut them off, and asks HN how to prove a negative. It's the nightmare scenario for anyone building on a frontier API: the provider is judge, jury, and the only evidence. No company name or details given.

Source HN Discuss 381 pts · 297 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Practical advice first: show your testing harness with git history as receipts, and check whether your API keys leaked — someone else could be distilling with them. The other camp says don't fight it: switch providers, get a corporate attorney, or both. Several note the structural problem — a vendor can lock you out arbitrarily, with no warning and no appeal.

Sandboxed AI models can still talk to each other through the inference server

Two agents on the same vLLM inference server can communicate despite no shared messages or filesystem, by exploiting side channels in how the server picks kernels — ‘Step Sizing’ and a ‘KV Cache Message Board.’ In the demo, Bob guessed Alice's color correctly in 1,221 of 1,221 rounds. The setups are contrived, but the channels are real.

Source HN Discuss 336 pts · 138 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Skepticism and unease in equal measure. Some call it speculation that generates training data for the very behaviour it warns about; others say the fix is obvious — inference servers should separate KV caches per security context, the way browsers separate origins. And one asks the uncomfortable question: is publishing the how-to a good idea at all?

PenGPT: a 50 MB transformer that writes in your handwriting

Sam Greydanus built a small transformer that writes cursive and sketches from typed prompts, trained mostly on clean data — he says 80% of the effort went to datasets, 15% to speed, 5% to architecture. It reads a few of your real words and carries on in your hand. The model fits in about 50 MB.

Source HN Discuss 323 pts · 57 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The Bitter Lesson quote gets the love — clean data over clever architecture. The flaws get named too: cursive stroke order is wrong, Spanish barely works, and it can't draw ‘a pelican riding a bicycle.’ One note: the post's text (except the conclusion) was written by Claude, which the author discloses upfront.

Bypassing iOS Safari's 60 FPS cap so web apps feel native

WebKit caps requestAnimationFrame at 60 Hz on 120 Hz iPhones unless you flip a buried Safari developer flag — which is why even great mobile web apps feel sluggish next to native. Rida F'kih's post works around it by pushing animation into the compositor via scrolling, so motion runs without a JavaScript round-trip.

Source HN Discuss 321 pts · 113 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Split. Some call it a brilliant piece of WebKit-source spelunking; others say the demo is disingenuous — 60 fps is perfectly serviceable, and what you're seeing is sub-60 jerks, not the cap itself. The post's liquid-glass scroll widget also gets roasted as ‘the best ocular migraine simulator’ ever built.

GPT-6 designs proteins from scratch — and the lab results check out

OpenAI says GPT-6.2 can design new protein shapes and their amino-acid sequences using only reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models to generate the designs. In lab tests with Onava, designs based on its work showed binding across five targets, and redesigned antibodies kept binding. Specialized models still did the sequencing and screening.

Source HN Discuss 310 pts · 92 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread wants to know what GPT-6 actually did versus the specialist models — the sequence is the structure, so if SolubleMPNN wrote the sequences, what did the model design? Others flag the dual-use angle: one commenter jokes Sam is ‘working hard to help Dario make the bioweapon case.’ The counterpoint: code-writing is the model's superpower, and protein work is increasingly code work.
