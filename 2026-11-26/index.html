The Mathocalypse continues: AI proves VP ≠ VNP, Riemann, and ω = 9/4

Scott Aaronson on a week of AI math proofs: OpenAI and Anthropic proved VP ≠ VNP, after the Alpöge/Anthropic Riemann Hypothesis proof — then OpenAI claimed ω, the matrix multiplication exponent, is exactly 9/4, with a matching lower bound, plus a faster FFT gate count. Ancient questions are getting answered outright. The shock isn't just the answers — it's that lower bounds, long thought near-impossible, are falling too.

Source HN Discuss 1760 pts · 1604 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The thread splits on whether the labs hold secret crypto-breaking capability. Skeptics say a breakthrough that big couldn't stay secret in a massive lab — whistleblowers would talk. Others note the cutting-edge math came from internal models the public never sees, and open-weight models may lag badly. The math itself isn't disputed; what it implies about hidden capability is.

1Password breach: backend systems hit, vaults untouched

An attacker got into some of 1Password's backend systems and copied limited customer account info — names and email addresses — from a subset of customers. Vault contents and the keys protecting them were not affected; 1Password says it has no access to your password or secret keys. Containment is done and clean versions restored. The pattern is the classic SaaS breach: one compromised laptop, code pushed, deployed.

Source HN Discuss 1546 pts · 665 comments
What HN commenters are saying

HN calls this the single most important SaaS attack vector — with enough employees, a compromised laptop approaches certainty. The thread then turns on AI-era shipping velocity: PR rates are up, but is the product actually improving? Several say 1Password's consumer product has gotten worse year over year while B2B gets the updates.

Anthropic's Riemann proof, now in a readable 300k lines of Lean

A much shorter, human-readable version of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis Lean verification — 300k lines instead of 17M, with a clear split between 'known math' and 'new math' and documentation throughout. You can verify it at home in a couple of hours; 18 independent validators have already run it, with a public leaderboard tracking them.

Source HN Discuss 497 pts · 309 comments
What HN commenters are saying

Praise for readability — the shorter proof should make the result digestible. The trust question recurs: nobody doubts the Lean code compiles, but running it yourself is the point. SETI@home nostalgia in the replies, plus one commenter building the proof out in their own theorem language.

NYT tells photographers: don't update Photoshop — the AI features will flag you

The New York Times is warning photographers not to update Photoshop, because its AI features risk getting photos flagged as AI-generated — a credibility killer for an authoritative source. The problem: you can't separate 'fully AI-generated' from 'minor color edit or cutout' once the pixels are final.

Source HN Discuss 495 pts · 414 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The creative-tools problem is real — an 'AI generated' tag on Instagram or Facebook is a death blow to credibility. The thread extends it to text: grammar-polish tools leave nearly as many fingerprints as full generation. AI detectors get debated — one says Pangram measures authorship, not understanding, and penalizes non-native speakers who had AI polish their own research.

7

Ubuntu's kernel security response is too slow, says oss-security

An oss-security post flags slow Ubuntu security response times on kernel patches. The stable kernel team ships 1,700–3,300 patches per release across seven concurrent kernels; Ubuntu maintains something like 100 kernel variants and has to backport the flood itself. Cherry-picking is the norm — and grsecurity keeps a running list of backports done wrong or missed entirely.

Source HN Discuss 351 pts · 195 comments
What HN commenters are saying

The stable maintainer's line gets quoted — take stable releases in order, don't cherry-pick, or you end up less secure. The thread relitigates monolithic vs microkernel and whether Ubuntu picked the wrong maintenance model. Debian gets it too: weekly kernel drops leave no time for adequate testing.

Defaults are good now; the l/1 and O/0 confusion that once broke a Fortran program is a solved problem for most people.

Source HN Discuss 240 pts · 150 comments
